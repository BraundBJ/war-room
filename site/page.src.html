How it works

Every player gets three numbers: actual value, value to a team and market value.

  • Actual value is the statistical bet: expected points above a replacement-level starter in this league's lineup (1QB, 2RB, 2WR, 1TE, 3FLEX), over the rest of 2026 and the next three seasons, times the chance of a healthy season. It counts the whole range of outcomes, not just the average: a breakout season counts in full, while a bad one costs nothing beyond sitting on your bench. That's why young players and breakout candidates near replacement level keep real value.
  • Value to a team re-simulates that team's lineup week by week with and without the player, drawing each player's season from his projected range. Empty lineup spots are filled from waivers at replacement level, so a player who wouldn't start is worth nothing to that team, and two good players are worth less than their sum if one would sit.
  • The scale runs from 0 to 10,000, with the league's most valuable player at 10,000. It is linear, so a 6,000 player is worth exactly two 3,000 players to a lineup that would start both.
  • Market value is what managers actually pay: FantasyCalc's dynasty values, built from real trades in leagues with the same format (QBs, team count, PPR). It is rescaled so its top 150 players add up to the same total as ours. "vs market" is our value minus the market's, in the viewing team's mode: positive means we like the player more than the market does.

Contender and rebuilder

Teams are labeled by points scored so far plus projected rest-of-season lineup strength: the top six are contenders. Each team's own value uses its mode.

The two modes share one scale: the top 150 players add up to the same total in both, so a player who is worth more to rebuilders shows a bigger rebuilder number. Young stars rank high in both; older players lose most of their rebuilder value.

Projections

Models trained on 2012 to 2025 from the same point in the season: the weeks played so far, the previous two seasons, age, experience and draft capital. They were tested on seasons they never saw, and weak forecasts are pulled toward the average automatically. Accuracy for next season (share of variation in PPG explained):

Ranges are 10th to 90th percentile outcomes. Past age 27 the models can only hold or lower a player's projection as he ages. 2030 and 2031 extend the 2029 projection with historical aging curves by position and age: how often a healthy player stays healthy, how often an injured one returns, and how PPG changes from one year to the next.

Age cliffs. The models alone underrate how fast older players fall off, because few players reach their mid 30s and the ones who do are survivors. So every fantasy-relevant season from 1999 to 2025 is used to measure, by position, age and scoring level, how often a player still has a healthy (8+ game) season one to five years later, with retirements and releases counted as misses, and how his PPG changes. From the cliff age (RB 27, WR and TE 29, QB 32) projections are pulled down to those rates whenever the model is higher. A starting RB at 29 has about a 35% chance of a healthy season three years later; at 33, about 12%.

Signals

The signal compares what a player's usage this season predicts for the rest of the year (target share, carry share, snap share, volume) with what his scoring alone predicts. Usage is stable week to week and leads future points; touchdowns and efficiency mostly regress. Positive means usage is ahead of scoring.

Partial games

Games a player left early with an injury (his snap share fell below half his norm and he then missed the next game or was on the injury report) are dropped from his averages. They still show on his trend chart, greyed out.

Opportunity

Return timing comes from about 2,500 absences since 2012: how many games players listed Out with each injury type actually missed. IR means at least four games; torn ACLs and Achilles end the season. A player is gaining when a teammate at his position who held a real role is out, and at risk once that teammate is likely back.

Picks

Each slot's value is the average lineup value that players taken there produced in their first three seasons, scored in this league's format. Slots that rest on few distinct players, like 1.01, are pulled toward a smooth curve across all slots, so a few great or terrible classes don't set the price. Picks add their value directly in trades rather than through the lineup simulation.

Season simulator

The rest of the regular season is played 6,000 times on the league's real schedule from Sleeper. Each team's weekly score is its projected lineup (the same week-by-week simulation as everything else), nudged a quarter of the way toward how it has actually scored, plus team-level uncertainty and the league's own week-to-week spread. Ties in record break on points for. The projected 2027 rookie draft order is the reverse of the final regular-season standings, so it doesn't account for playoff results or lotteries.

Team rankings

Now value adds up contender values of each roster's players and picks. Future value adds up rebuilder values of players and picks, so draft capital counts toward a team's future.

Trends

A player is trending up when his last three full games beat his earlier games by 5 or more points of target share (carry share for RBs) or 10 or more points of snap share, and down for the reverse. New opportunity lists players whose teammate at the position is out, or whose role just jumped, ranked by the size of the change, so a backup who suddenly starts ranks above a star who added a few targets.

Your league

Use the League button to load any Sleeper league by username or league ID; the share link opens the page on that league. Everything league-specific is rebuilt in your browser from live Sleeper data: lineup slots (including superflex), team count, rosters, picks, schedule and trades. Replacement level comes from that league's actual waiver wire. Player projections are the same for every league and assume full PPR, so half-PPR scoring and TE premium aren't modeled.

Trade scores

Each side's score runs from −100 to +100: net value gained divided by the larger of what it sent and what it received. +100 means value back for nothing, 0 an even swap. The overall number is half the gap between the two teams' scores, each in its own mode.

League trades

Placeholder trades are filtered out: round trips between the same two teams within three weeks, FAAB-only swaps, startup pick swaps, and the McCarthy for Carson Beck trade.
